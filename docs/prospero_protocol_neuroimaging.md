## Section 3 — Methods

### 15. Review question

**Primary question:** In adults with Functional Neurological Disorder (FND) compared to healthy controls, what brain regions show consistent structural and functional abnormalities as identified by coordinate-based (ALE) meta-analysis of neuroimaging studies?

**Secondary questions:**

1. Do functional (task-based fMRI, PET, SPECT) and structural (VBM, cortical thickness) meta-analyses converge on overlapping brain regions (conjunction analysis)?
2. Do brain abnormalities differ by FND subtype (motor FND, psychogenic non-epileptic seizures [PNES], functional sensory symptoms)?
3. How do updated findings (2015–present) compare to the original Boeckle et al. (2016) motor conversion ALE results?
4. Are findings robust to methodological moderators (imaging modality, coordinate system, scanner field strength, statistical thresholding)?

**Structured PECO formulation:**

The PECO framework (Population, Exposure, Comparator, Outcome) is adopted in preference to PICO, as this is an observational (case-control) meta-analysis examining brain correlates of a clinical condition — not an intervention effectiveness review. PECO is the recommended framework for systematic reviews of observational studies of etiology per COSMOS-E guidance (Dekkers et al., 2019, *PLoS Med*) and Morgan et al. (2018, *Environ Int*). Munn et al. (2018, *BMC Med Res Methodol*) classify this review type as "etiology and/or risk," for which PEO/PECO is the appropriate question framework.

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **P** — Population | Adults (≥18 years at time of neuroimaging) with a confirmed diagnosis of Functional Neurological Disorder (FND) / conversion disorder / functional neurological symptom disorder, across any FND subtype (motor, sensory, seizures, mixed). Diagnostic criteria include DSM-IV (300.11), DSM-5/DSM-5-TR, ICD-10 (F44.x), ICD-11 (6B60), ILAE criteria for PNES, Stone & Carson (2010) rule-in criteria, or clinically established positive signs. Pre-specify accepted criteria per subtype. |
| **E** — Exposure | Having a diagnosis of FND. In this observational case-control design, the "exposure" is the clinical condition itself — the presence of FND in cases compared to its absence in controls. |
| **C** — Comparator | **Primary:** Healthy control participants without FND or other major neurological/psychiatric disorders, recruited from community or non-clinical settings. **Secondary (subgroup):** Clinical controls — e.g., epilepsy controls for PNES studies, organic movement disorder controls for functional movement disorder studies. Clinical controls are analysed separately and not pooled with healthy controls. |
| **O** — Outcome | Convergent spatial abnormalities in brain structure and function, identified through coordinate-based (ALE) meta-analysis. Specifically: (a) **Functional:** task-based activation differences (fMRI, PET, SPECT) reported as peak coordinates in MNI or Talairach space; (b) **Structural:** grey matter volume/density differences (VBM), cortical thickness differences, reported as peak coordinates. White matter / DTI coordinates are not comparable to grey-matter ALE and are analysed via narrative synthesis only. Outcome is the spatial convergence (or divergence) of reported coordinates across studies, quantified by ALE cluster-level family-wise error corrected statistics. |

### 18. Condition or domain being studied

Functional Neurological Disorder (FND), also known as conversion disorder or functional neurological symptom disorder — a common condition characterised by neurological symptoms incompatible with recognised neurological disease, where the symptom is genuine and causes significant distress or impairment (DSM-5; ICD-11 6B60). FND encompasses motor subtypes (functional movement disorders, functional weakness, functional gait disorder), seizure subtype (psychogenic non-epileptic seizures / functional dissociative seizures), sensory subtypes (functional visual, vestibular, sensory symptoms), and mixed presentations.

This review examines the neuroimaging correlates of FND — specifically, whether convergent brain structural and functional abnormalities can be identified through coordinate-based meta-analysis, extending and updating the prior ALE meta-analysis by Boeckle et al. (2016) which was limited to motor conversion disorder and pre-August 2015 literature.

### 19. Participants/population

#### 19.1 Inclusion criteria

- Adults aged ≥18 years at the time of neuroimaging.
- Diagnosis of FND / conversion disorder / functional neurological symptom disorder established by:
  - **Motor FND:** DSM-IV (300.11), DSM-5/DSM-5-TR, ICD-10 (F44.4–F44.7), ICD-11 (6B60), or clinically established positive signs (e.g., Hoover's sign, tremor entrainment, dragging gait).
  - **PNES:** ILAE criteria (LaFrance et al., 2013), DSM-5, or video-EEG confirmation.
  - **Sensory FND:** DSM-5/ICD-11 criteria for functional sensory symptoms.
  - **Mixed/unspecified FND:** DSM-IV/5, ICD-10/11 criteria.
- Studies reporting neuroimaging data comparing FND patients to a control group.
- Studies reporting peak activation/volume-difference coordinates in standardised space (MNI or Talairach).

#### 19.2 Exclusion criteria

- Paediatric samples (<18 at imaging), unless adult subgroup data are separately extractable.
- Diagnoses based solely on self-report without clinical evaluation.
- Studies that do not report coordinates in MNI or Talairach space (e.g., ROI-only analyses, whole-brain maps without peak coordinates) — these are recorded and included in narrative synthesis but excluded from ALE.
- EEG, MEG, fNIRS studies (no spatial coordinates in MNI/Talairach space).
- Case reports or case series with <10 participants.
- Studies examining FND as a comorbidity of a separately-targeted primary neurological condition (e.g., FND symptoms in established epilepsy cohort) without FND-specific subsample reporting.

### 20. Intervention(s), exposure(s)

This review concerns an **exposure** (clinical condition), not an intervention. The exposure is having a diagnosis of FND. In the observational case-control design of included studies, cases are adults with FND and controls are healthy individuals without FND.

The exposure is characterised by:

1. **FND subtype:** motor FND (functional movement disorder, functional weakness, functional gait), PNES (functional/dissociative seizures), sensory FND (functional visual, vestibular, sensory), mixed/unspecified. Subtype is recorded and used as a moderator in subgroup analysis.
2. **Diagnostic criteria:** the specific diagnostic system used (DSM-IV, DSM-5, ICD-10, ICD-11, clinical positive signs, ILAE for PNES). This is recorded and treated as a sensitivity variable.
3. **FND vs. healthy controls (primary)** vs. **FND vs. clinical controls (secondary/subgroup):** the comparator type is recorded and clinical-control comparisons are analysed separately.

### 21. Comparator(s)/control

**Primary comparator:** Healthy controls — individuals without FND or other major neurological/psychiatric disorders, recruited from community, workplace, university, or general-practice non-clinical samples. As defined by each included study's control recruitment and screening procedures.

**Secondary comparator (subgroup analysis, not pooled with primary):**
- Neurological disease controls (e.g., epilepsy controls for PNES studies; organic movement disorder controls for functional movement disorder studies).
- Psychiatric controls (e.g., depression, anxiety).
- Mixed control groups in which healthy and patient controls are not separately reported are excluded from ALE.

### 22. Types of study to be included

#### 22.1 Eligible designs

- Case-control studies (primary design) comparing FND patients to healthy controls using neuroimaging.
- Cross-sectional studies with explicit healthy-control comparison group.
- Within-subject designs (e.g., affected vs. unaffected limb in motor FND) — analysed as a separate ALE.
- Baseline data from intervention trials, where the comparison of FND cases vs. healthy controls at baseline is reported.

#### 22.2 Ineligible designs

- Case reports and case series (n < 10 per FND group).
- Narrative reviews, systematic reviews, meta-analyses (used only for citation tracking).
- Editorials, commentaries, letters.
- Qualitative studies.
- Animal studies.
- Studies without a control group (single-arm studies) — except within-subject motor FND designs comparing affected vs. unaffected sides, which are eligible for a separate ALE.

#### 22.3 Coordinate reporting requirement

Studies must report peak coordinates (local maxima) of brain activation/volume-difference clusters in MNI or Talairach stereotactic space. Studies reporting only:
- Region-of-interest (ROI) results without whole-brain coordinates
- Statistical maps without extracted peak coordinates
- Cluster-level descriptions without numeric coordinates

are recorded but excluded from ALE. They are included in narrative synthesis.

### 23. Context

International; no geographical restriction. Country of data collection is recorded and treated as an exploratory moderator.

### 24. Main outcome(s)

**Primary outcome:** Spatial convergence of brain activation/difference coordinates across included neuroimaging studies, quantified by Activation Likelihood Estimation (ALE).

Two primary ALE meta-analyses are conducted:

1. **Functional ALE:** convergent brain activation differences (FND > controls and controls > FND) from task-based fMRI, PET, and SPECT studies.
2. **Structural ALE:** convergent grey matter volume/density differences (FND > controls and controls > FND) from VBM and cortical thickness studies.

Each ALE produces:
- ALE statistical maps (cluster-level FWE-corrected, p < 0.05; cluster-forming threshold p < 0.001 uncorrected).
- Peak coordinates, cluster sizes, and anatomical labels (AAL3 or Harvard-Oxford atlas) for significant clusters.
- Laterality index where applicable.

**Conjunction analysis:** A formal conjunction of functional and structural ALE maps identifies regions of overlap between functional activation and structural grey-matter differences (via NiMARE conjunction or minimum-statistic approach).

#### 24.1 Timing of outcome measurement

The "outcome" in this PECO framework is the cross-sectional brain difference between FND cases and controls at the time of neuroimaging. All included studies are cross-sectional or case-control; no longitudinal treatment-outcome studies are included.

### 25. Additional outcome(s)

1. **Subtype-specific ALEs:** where k ≥ 10 experiments per subtype, separate ALE meta-analyses for motor FND, PNES, and sensory FND.
2. **Within-subject motor ALE:** affected vs. unaffected limb/body side comparisons in motor FND studies (separate ALE, as per Boeckle et al. 2016).
3. **Modality-specific ALEs:** where k permits, separate ALEs for fMRI-only, PET/SPECT-only, VBM-only.
4. **Conjunction analysis:** overlap between functional and structural ALE maps.
5. **Functional decoding / characterisation:** post-hoc functional characterisation of significant ALE clusters using the Neurosynth database or NeuroVault meta-analytic co-activation maps, to interpret the cognitive/behavioural associations of convergent brain regions.
6. **Comparison with Boeckle et al. (2016):** direct overlay of updated functional ALE results with the original Boeckle 2016 motor-conversion ALE map (if coordinates/maps are obtainable from authors or publication supplement).

### 26. Data extraction (selection and coding)

#### 26.1 Screening process

- **Stage 1 — title/abstract:** two reviewers independently screen all records. An LLM-assisted screening pipeline (validated on two independent benchmarks: Boeckle et al. 2016 gold standard, 25/25 sensitivity; Ludwig et al. 2018 gold standard, 15/15 sensitivity; 50-record human-LLM comparison with 0 LLM misses) is used as a high-sensitivity pre-filter to prioritise records for dual human review. All LLM-included and LLM-uncertain records are dual-screened by humans. LLM-excluded records are spot-checked (random 10% sample) by a human reviewer to verify exclusion accuracy. Reviewers are blinded to each other's decisions until reconciliation. A pilot of the first 200 records establishes inter-rater agreement (Cohen's κ); agreement < 0.6 triggers re-calibration.
- **Stage 2 — full text:** two reviewers independently assess full texts. The critical full-text screening question is whether the study reports coordinates in MNI or Talairach space. Reasons for exclusion at full-text stage are recorded for the PRISMA flow.
- **Conflict resolution:** discussion to consensus; unresolved disagreements are arbitrated by a third reviewer.

#### 26.2 Extraction process

- Two reviewers independently extract data using a piloted, structured extraction form (Excel or REDCap, archived on OSF). A pilot extraction on five included studies precedes full extraction.
- Disagreements are resolved by discussion; if unresolved, by a third reviewer.
- Authors of included studies are contacted by email for missing coordinates, diagnostic criteria details, or subgroup data. A reminder is sent after 4 weeks.

#### 26.3 Extracted variables

- **Bibliographic:** authors, year, journal, country of data collection, language.
- **Sample characteristics:** total n, n per group (FND, control), % female per group, mean age (SD) per group, recruitment setting, FND subtype, diagnostic criteria applied.
- **Clinical characteristics:** FND subtype (motor / PNES / sensory / mixed), duration of FND symptoms, comorbidities (depression %, anxiety %, PTSD %), medication status.
- **Imaging parameters:** modality (fMRI / PET / SPECT / VBM / cortical thickness / DTI), scanner field strength (1.5T / 3T / 7T), task paradigm (for fMRI), tracer (for PET/SPECT), smoothing kernel (FWHM), statistical threshold used in original study, software platform (SPM, FSL, AFNI, etc.).
- **Coordinate data:** all reported peak coordinates (local maxima) for each contrast of interest (FND > controls; controls > FND), coordinate space (MNI / Talairach), cluster size if reported, Z/t/F statistic if reported, anatomical label as reported.
- **Within-subject designs:** coordinates for affected > unaffected and unaffected > affected contrasts.
- **Risk of bias inputs:** as required for the neuroimaging-adapted Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (see Field 27).

#### 26.4 Coordinate processing

- Talairach coordinates are converted to MNI using the Brett transform (1999) or the Lancaster et al. (2007) transform, with the conversion method documented.
- If a study reports both MNI and Talairach coordinates, the MNI coordinates are used.
- If a study reports coordinates from multiple statistical thresholds, the most liberal threshold that survives whole-brain correction is used (to avoid arbitrary threshold-dependent coordinate selection).
- If a study reports separate coordinates for multiple task contrasts (within the same modality), all contrasts meeting inclusion criteria are included as separate experiments in the ALE (with appropriate handling of non-independence via subtractive ALE or sensitivity analysis).

### 27. Risk of bias (quality) assessment

**Tool:** Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for case-control studies, adapted for neuroimaging studies, supplemented with neuroimaging-specific quality criteria.

**Justification:** The NOS is used for direct comparability with Boeckle et al. (2016), which used NOS. An adapted version with neuroimaging-specific items is applied, as the standard NOS does not address imaging methodological quality (e.g., field strength homogeneity, motion correction, coordinate reporting quality).

#### 27.1 NOS domains (case-control version, 9 stars across 3 domains)

1. **Selection (max 4 stars):**
   - Case definition adequate (clinically confirmed FND diagnosis) — 1 star
   - Representativeness of cases (consecutive or community vs. convenience sample) — 1 star
   - Selection of controls (community/healthy vs. hospital/staff) — 1 star
   - Definition of controls (no history of neurological/psychiatric disease) — 1 star
2. **Comparability (max 2 stars):**
   - Cases and controls matched on age and sex — 1 star
   - Cases and controls matched on additional relevant variable(s) (e.g., education, IQ) — 1 star
3. **Exposure (max 3 stars — adapted for neuroimaging):**
   - Imaging modality and parameters adequately described and standardised between groups — 1 star
   - Same scanner and acquisition protocol for cases and controls — 1 star
   - Ascertainment of imaging quality (motion correction, adequate coverage, QC documented) — 1 star

**Threshold:** ≥7 stars = high quality; 5–6 = moderate; <5 = low.

#### 27.2 Neuroimaging-specific quality supplement

In addition to NOS, the following are assessed and recorded:

| Criterion | Assessed as |
|---|---|
| Coordinate space clearly stated (MNI or Talairach) | Yes / No |
| Whole-brain analysis (not ROI-only) | Yes / No |
| Statistical correction method reported | FWE / FDR / uncorrected / not reported |
| Motion artefact addressed (for fMRI) | Yes / No / N/A |
| Sample size ≥20 per group | Yes / No |
| Diagnostic criteria for FND explicitly stated | Yes / No |
| Control group screening described | Yes / No |

These items do not contribute to the NOS score but are reported descriptively and used in sensitivity analyses.

#### 27.3 Assessment process

- Two reviewers independently complete NOS + neuroimaging supplement for each included study.
- A pilot on five studies establishes inter-rater agreement (target κ ≥ 0.6); discrepant ratings are discussed before proceeding.
- A third reviewer arbitrates unresolved disagreements.
- Inter-rater reliability statistics are reported in the final manuscript.

#### 27.4 Integration of RoB into synthesis

1. **Sensitivity analysis:** primary ALEs are re-run excluding studies with NOS < 5 (low quality).
2. **Subgroup comparison:** where k permits, separate ALEs for high-quality (NOS ≥ 7) vs. moderate-quality (NOS 5–6) studies.
3. **GRADE certainty:** RoB contributes to the GRADE assessment for each outcome (see Field 28.5). Observational evidence starts at Low certainty; may be upgraded for large effects, dose-response, or plausible confounding direction.

Risk of bias is NOT used to weight studies in ALE (ALE weights by sample size / within-study variance, not by quality). Studies are NOT excluded from primary ALE on RoB grounds alone; exclusion is restricted to the pre-specified sensitivity analysis.

### 28. Strategy for data synthesis

#### 28.1 Overview

Quantitative synthesis is conducted via coordinate-based ALE meta-analysis, separately for functional and structural neuroimaging. A conjunction analysis tests overlap between the two. Where pre-specified minimum experiment counts are not met, narrative synthesis is conducted per the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) guidance (Campbell et al., 2020).

#### 28.2 Software

- **NiMARE** (Python; https://nimare.readthedocs.io/) — primary ALE engine.
- **GingerALE** (BrainMap; as sensitivity comparison, if available at execution time).
- Python 3.11+ with NumPy, SciPy, nibabel, nilearn.
- All analysis code archived on OSF with fixed random seeds for reproducibility.

#### 28.3 ALE model specification

- **Algorithm:** Revised ALE (Eickhoff et al., 2012) as implemented in NiMARE.
- **Kernel:** Study-specific FWHM derived from empirical neuroimaging data (Eickhoff et al., 2009), not fixed-width Gaussian.
- **Null distribution:** 10,000 Monte Carlo permutation tests (R-Kinkowski algorithm or voxel-level inference).
- **Cluster-level inference:** Cluster-forming threshold p < 0.001 (uncorrected); cluster-level FWE correction at p < 0.05.
- **Voxel-level inference:** Reported as supplementary (voxel-level FWE p < 0.05) where applicable.
- **Minimum experiments:** ≥20 experiments per ALE is the target for adequate power (Eickhoff et al., 2016); ≥17 is the hard floor. Below 17, only narrative synthesis is conducted.
- **Talairach-to-MNI conversion:** Lancaster et al. (2007) transform (icbm2tal) as implemented in NiMARE.
- **Directionality:** Separate ALEs for FND > controls and controls > FND. Within-subject motor FND: affected > unaffected and unaffected > affected.

#### 28.4 Conjunction analysis

- Conjunction of functional and structural ALE maps is computed via the minimum-statistic approach: a voxel is significant in the conjunction if it is significant in both the functional ALE and the structural ALE (Nichols et al., 2005).
- This identifies brain regions showing both functional activation differences and structural volume differences between FND and controls.

#### 28.5 Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

**Pre-specified subgroup ALEs** (where k ≥ 17 per subgroup):

| Subgroup | Comparison |
|---|---|
| FND subtype | Motor FND vs. PNES vs. sensory FND (separate ALEs) |
| Modality | fMRI-only vs. PET/SPECT-only (functional); VBM-only (structural) |
| Comparator type | FND vs. healthy controls only (excluding clinical control comparisons) |
| Recency | Pre-2015 vs. 2015–present (to compare with Boeckle et al. 2016 timeframe) |

**Pre-specified sensitivity analyses:**

1. Excluding studies with NOS < 5 (low quality).
2. Excluding studies using 1.5T scanners only.
3. Excluding studies with < 20 participants per group.
4. Excluding studies that do not report whole-brain correction.
5. Excluding studies using Talairach space (MNI-only ALE).
6. Leave-one-study-out analysis (jackknife) to assess robustness.
7. Using GingerALE instead of NiMARE (if available, for software replication).

#### 28.6 DTI / white matter

DTI/white matter studies are NOT included in ALE. White-matter coordinate distributions (fractional anisotropy, mean diffusivity) are not directly comparable to grey-matter ALE kernels. DTI findings are reported via narrative synthesis, grouped by measure (FA, MD) and FND subtype.

#### 28.7 Resting-state fMRI

Resting-state fMRI studies that report seed-based or ICA-derived coordinates in MNI/Talairach space are eligible for a separate ALE (if k ≥ 17). Studies reporting only connectivity matrices, graph metrics, or network-level statistics without peak coordinates are included in narrative synthesis only.

#### 28.8 Publication bias

- **Funnel plots** of ALE values per experiment (if k ≥ 20) — interpreted with caution due to the non-standard effect-size distribution of coordinate-based data.
- **Egger's regression test** (if applicable given the coordinate-based data structure).
- Comparison of ALE results with and without grey literature (conference abstracts, dissertations) to assess potential publication bias.
- Acknowledged limitation: standard publication bias assessment tools are designed for effect-size meta-analyses, not coordinate-based meta-analyses. The limited applicability of these tools to ALE is discussed transparently.

#### 28.9 Minimum k thresholds

| Analysis | Minimum experiments (k) | If below threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Primary functional ALE | k ≥ 17 (target ≥ 20) | Narrative synthesis (SWiM) |
| Primary structural ALE | k ≥ 17 (target ≥ 20) | Narrative synthesis (SWiM) |
| Subgroup ALE by subtype | k ≥ 17 per subgroup | Narrative within-subgroup |
| Subgroup ALE by modality | k ≥ 17 per subgroup | Narrative within-subgroup |
| Conjunction analysis | Both functional and structural ALEs must be significant | Not conducted |
| Funnel plot / asymmetry test | k ≥ 20 | Not conducted |

#### 28.10 Certainty of evidence (GRADE)

GRADE is applied to each primary outcome (functional ALE; structural ALE). Observational evidence starts at Low certainty. The five GRADE domains are assessed:

1. **Risk of bias** (per NOS synthesis).
2. **Inconsistency** (degree of overlap between study coordinate sets; I² analogue not available for ALE — assessed qualitatively).
3. **Indirectness** (population, comparator, outcome directness).
4. **Imprecision** (number of experiments; width of ALE clusters; prediction of power per Eickhoff et al. 2016).
5. **Publication bias** (per §28.8).

Certainty may be upgraded for large spatial convergence effects or dose-response patterns (e.g., greater convergence in studies with larger sample sizes). Rated as High / Moderate / Low / Very Low and reported in a Summary of Findings table.

### 29. Analysis of subgroups or subsets

Pre-specified subgroup and moderator analyses, in descending priority:

**A. Primary subgroups:**

1. **FND subtype** (motor FND / PNES / sensory FND / mixed) — separate ALEs where k permits.
2. **Modality** (fMRI / PET / SPECT / VBM / cortical thickness) — separate ALEs where k permits.
3. **Direction** (FND > controls / controls > FND) — separate ALEs by design.
4. **Comparator type** (healthy controls / clinical controls) — clinical controls analysed separately.

**B. Secondary subgroups:**

5. **Scanner field strength** (1.5T / 3T / 7T) — sensitivity analysis.
6. **Diagnostic criteria** (DSM-5/ICD-11 vs. older criteria) — sensitivity analysis.
7. **Recruitment setting** (tertiary neurology clinic vs. community/mixed).
8. **Year of publication** (pre-2015 vs. 2015–present) — to compare with Boeckle et al. (2016) search window.

**C. Exploratory:**

9. **Within-subject motor FND** (affected vs. unaffected side) — separate ALE.
10. **Resting-state fMRI** coordinates (if separately reportable).
11. **Medication status** (medication-free vs. medication-naïve vs. unspecified).

### 30. Type and method of review

Systematic review with coordinate-based (ALE) quantitative meta-analysis.

### 39. Any additional information

This protocol updates and extends the scope of Boeckle et al. (2016, *BMC Psychiatry*, 16:195) beyond motor conversion disorder to all FND subtypes (motor, PNES, sensory, mixed), extends the search window from August 2015 to present, and adds a structural neuroimaging ALE track (VBM, cortical thickness) searched from inception. The prior ALE by Boeckle et al. used GingerALE 2.3.1 on 12 functional neuroimaging studies of motor conversion disorder (187 subjects). The present review uses current NiMARE software, covers both functional and structural neuroimaging, includes all FND subtypes, and follows PRISMA 2020 guidelines with prospective PROSPERO registration — a methodological improvement over the original.

The Mavroudis et al. (2024, *Iran J Psychiatry Behav Sci*, 18(4):e148266) voxel-wise meta-analysis used ES-SDM software on VBM studies only. The present review uses ALE methodology (different algorithm, different software) and broadens the scope to include functional neuroimaging, providing a complementary and more comprehensive synthesis.

Anticipated limitations acknowledged at registration:

- **Coordinate availability:** Not all neuroimaging studies report peak coordinates in standardised space. Studies reporting only ROI analyses or statistical maps without coordinates are included in narrative synthesis but excluded from ALE, potentially reducing the ALE experiment count.
- **Heterogeneity of FND:** FND is a heterogeneous condition. Pooling across subtypes (motor, PNES, sensory) may obscure subtype-specific patterns. Subtype-specific ALEs are planned where k permits, but power may be insufficient for all subtypes.
- **Coordinate-based limitations:** ALE captures spatial convergence of peak coordinates but does not account for effect sizes, within-study variance, or extent of activation. The method assumes that peak coordinates are representative of the underlying activation pattern, which may not always hold.
- **Publication bias in coordinate-based meta-analysis:** Standard publication bias tools (funnel plots, Egger's test) are designed for effect-size meta-analyses and have limited applicability to coordinate-based data. This is acknowledged as a methodological limitation.
- **DTI exclusion from ALE:** White-matter DTI coordinates are not comparable to grey-matter ALE kernels and are analysed via narrative synthesis only, limiting the comprehensiveness of the quantitative synthesis for white-matter findings.
- **Software dependency:** ALE results may differ between software implementations (NiMARE vs. GingerALE). A sensitivity analysis using GingerALE is planned where feasible.
